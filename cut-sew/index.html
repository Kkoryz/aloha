Cut and Sew Hawaiian Shirts, From Raw Materials to Finished Garments

We make your aloha shirts from raw materials rather than printing onto blank shirts. Your custom patterns, the fabric you select and the sewing itself all stay under the control of one team, working in our own factory in Guangzhou, Guangdong, China.

Print Matching, Cutting Layouts & Fabric Allowances

At sampling, we agree with you where the main motif sits on the chest, pocket and back, and only then do we finalise the cutting marker.

Large repeats, one-way motifs and matched pockets or plackets take 10–15% more fabric so the panels stay aligned and face the same way. That allowance is already in your unit price, and your quote shows fabric consumption per shirt so you can compare layouts.

We build the marker around your repeat, which runs up to 64 cm for screen printing or the full 150 cm printable width for digital printing.

Collars, front plackets, pockets and hems are matched with no visible break, and each pocket is cut at its exact position in the repeat. Matched panels are cut one ply at a time instead of stacked, and your quote already covers this slower cutting.

In-House Production & Direct Ordering Options

Order quantities

Orders start at 50 pieces per style in each colour, which suits small and growing labels. For a first trial, we agree the quantity with you case by case within our production capacity.

Product development

Through Product Development, we offer OEM/ODM and wash-tested contrast panels. The fabrics we pair are within about 40 GSM and 2% shrinkage of each other, and remaining shrinkage is no more than 3% after three home washes.

Direct factory relationship

Our factory has been running since 2011 and handles sublimation in-house. We sell B2B only, through this website and our team, and have no online shop or third-party marketplace store.

Pattern Creation, Revisions & Size Grading

We develop your base-size pattern and revise it before any grading begins, so every change to the fit has to go in before you approve the base size. Our patterns follow the sizing standards of major fashion labels as well as international sizing standards.

Approve the base-size fit before grading.

  1. Create the pattern

    Start from procedural drawings and technical specifications, or reverse-engineer a physical sample or reference shirt.

  2. Revise measurements and details

    Pattern makers rework the pattern at every revision round; any shirt measurement can be adjusted.

  3. Approve base-size fit

    Fully approve the base-size fit and each detail; make every fit change at this stage.

  4. Grade the approved pattern

    Grade across the full size range, from XXS to 5XL, keeping sizing consistent between production runs.

Back Pleats & Yokes for Your Preferred Fit

Where the main motif of your back print sits decides which back construction keeps the artwork intact.

  • Plain back

    Fit and print result
    Keeps a large back print in one unbroken piece.

  • Centre box pleat

    Fit and print result
    A 2–3 cm pleat gives you relaxed room across the back. The shoulders stay narrow, and the shoulder seam stays true to your size chart.

  • Twin side pleats

    Fit and print result
    Two 1.5 cm pleats near the shoulder blades give you more back room without widening the shoulders you specified.

  • Back yoke

    Fit and print result
    We make each of these back constructions with or without a yoke.

Fabric, Trim & Button Selection with Material Checks

  • Your fabric allowance

    What we supply or check
    Allow about 1.6–1.8 m for each standard short-sleeve shirt at a usable width of 140–150 cm. We work out your exact consumption, including the matching allowance where it applies. Send 5–8% extra before cutting. Sticking to a single dye lot means lots don't get mixed, and roll labels keep everything traceable.

  • Incoming fabric

    What we supply or check
    Before anything is used, we inspect shade, direction and quality and wash-test for shrinkage. Within 2 business days, we report any shortages, defects, shade variation or shrinkage over 3%. You then choose between replacement fabric and cutting around the flaws using the allowance. We also sort shade lots so each shirt stays consistent, or preshrink the fabric before cutting.

  • Sourced materials

    What we supply or check
    Through Fabric Sourcing, we check fabrics, trims and accessories for quality, consistency and compliance. We offer bases from 60 to 200 GSM matched to your printing method, and we send you swatches so you can approve the drape and hand.

  • Buttons and supplied trims

    What we supply or check
    Through Custom Buttons, we supply coconut, wood, shell or resin buttons from 14L to 24L (about 9 to 15 mm). If you supply your own trims, send them with 3% spares before cutting. Buttons need two or four holes at least 1.5 mm wide. We report delivery shortages or defects within 2 business days and sample-test the attachment. Branded tooling is quoted separately, and we keep the moulds for 3 years.

  • Reverse-print cotton

    What we supply or check
    Through Hawaiian Fabrics, we make reverse-print shirts with the printed face turned inside, which gives the outside a muted look. We use 100% cotton poplin at 110–130 gsm, once you approve strike-offs of both faces and a sample of the outer effect.

  • Trim scheduling

    What we supply or check
    Through Trims & Accessories, sourcing takes 7 to 15 days. Trims are ordered along with the fabric and arrive at least 3 days before cutting, so we can check them against the approved card. Cutting doesn't start until all the fabric and trims are in.

Industrial Equipment for Precise Cutting & Sewing

We cut on industrial cutting machines and sew on single-needle lockstitch lines fitted with specialised presser feet.

During sampling, we pick the folders and hemming attachments that suit your fabric and your cuff and hem widths. Our Bulk Production floor covers 5,800 square metres. It holds 13 production lines, 6 automatic cutting lines and more than 580 sewing machines.

When your timeline requires it, we run 24/7. Through Custom Printing, we print all-over repeats on rolled fabric before cutting, so the pattern matches continuously across collars, plackets, pockets and hems. Engineered or placement designs are printed on cut panels before sewing, including sublimation transfer on polyester.

This puts the motif in the same position on every shirt.

Stitch Specifications, Seam Reinforcement & Quality Checks

  • Seam finish

    Construction and acceptance requirement
    At sampling, we choose French seams where sheer fabric shows the inside, or overlocking for a lighter, softer seam. We overlock cotton, polyester blends, rayon and other lightweight fabrics. We also offer double-needle stitching for casual shirts, including twill and chambray.

  • Reinforcement

    Construction and acceptance requirement
    Armholes, side seams, pocket corners, yokes and sleeve openings that take stress get stronger thread, double stitching, extra fabric layers or bar tacks, depending on the construction. Bar tacks go at pocket corners, sleeve plackets, side vents and bottom openings.

  • Stitch density

    Construction and acceptance requirement
    Main seams run at 10 to 14 stitches per inch and topstitching at 8 to 12 SPI, and we set the exact specification at sampling. Lightweight rayon is sewn at 12 SPI. Each automatic buttonhole has 80 to 100 stitches.

  • Fine-fabric seams

    Construction and acceptance requirement
    Lightweight rayon gets 1 cm overlocked or French seams, tested to ISO 13936-2 with a seam opening of no more than 6 mm. Silk and very fine fabrics can also have flat-felled side seams or narrow 0.6 cm French seams, which finish flat with little bulk.

  • Inspection limits

    Construction and acceptance requirement
    Through Quality Control, we check fabric on arrival and then check cutting and sewing. We carry out random checks and a full final inspection against your approved sample. Any shirt outside ±1 cm on chest width, body length and sleeves, or ±0.5 cm on collars, cuffs and small points, is rejected and then reworked or replaced. You're welcome to bring in SGS or BV for inspection. Tested fabrics and trims carry OEKO-TEX Standard 100, organic cotton styles carry GOTS, cotton comes through Better Cotton (BCI), and our factory holds ISO 9001 and WRAP.

Construction Choices for Collars, Fronts & Hems

  • Custom sewing details

    What it changes
    You set the stitch and seam finishes, collar and cuff construction, button attachment, pockets, reinforcement, pressing, trimming and packing.

  • Camp collar

    What it changes
    We cut the collar in one piece with no collar stand and join it to a 3.5 cm self-faced front. Then we turn and press it and topstitch at 0.6 cm, so it falls open and lies flat.

  • Front opening

    What it changes
    You choose a folded-back camp facing, a separate sewn-on placket, a concealed-button fabric fly or a French front. The French front folds under with no separate placket seam, which keeps large artwork intact. Sewn-on or concealed plackets give a more structured front.

  • Rayon handling

    What it changes
    Fine needles in sizes 9 to 11, low thread tension and a walking foot stop puckering. Straight-grain facings backed with light fusible or soft non-fused interlining keep plackets flat before edge-stitching and gentle, low-heat steam pressing.

  • Hem options

    What it changes
    You choose a straight hem with 4–6 cm side vents, a curved shirt-tail hem or a straight hem without vents. Each one is finished with a 1 cm double-turned narrow hem. Vented straight hems sit level at the hip when worn untucked, and curved hems suit tucked or loose wear.

  • Interlining

    What it changes
    At sampling, you choose standard lightweight fusible, which gives crisp cotton shape without stiffness. For drapey rayon, you choose soft sewn-in non-fused interlining or none at all, which gives an unstructured, softer roll.

From Quote to Bulk Production: Timing & Payment Terms

  1. Agree the quote

    As Our Process sets out, we quote first from your brief or tech pack, and sampling starts once you've agreed the price.

  2. Approve the production sample

    Before we cut bulk, you approve a pre-production sample sewn in the actual fabric, with your logo applied and its measurements checked against your size chart.

  3. Confirm and fund production

    You pay a 50% deposit when you confirm the order. Once you've approved the sample and we've received the deposit, bulk production takes 35 to 40 days, depending on order size and season.

  4. Allow for garment dyeing

    If you choose Garment Dyeing, it adds about 5 to 7 days for lab dips and 7 to 10 days for bulk dyeing. That dyeing runs in our own factory, on PFD shirts we sew.

  5. Assemble and finish shirts

    We cut collar, sleeve, front and back panels for each size and assemble them. Then we add buttonholes, buttons and neck labels, trim the threads, press each shirt and polybag it individually.

  6. Release the finished order

    After inline checks, measurement checks and a full final inspection, you pay the remaining 50%, and then we release the shirts for shipment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you assemble aloha shirts from pre-cut panels we supply, rather than handling the whole manufacturing process?

Yes. We sew the panels you supply and add your trims, buttons and labels, or ours. Then we finish, press and pack the shirts in our factory. Bundle and label the sets by size and shade lot so each shirt's panels come from the same lay, and send 3% spare panels per size. We count and inspect each delivery and report any shortages or defects within 2 business days, before sewing starts.

Your lines run lockstitch machines. How do you sew knit or stretch aloha shirts so the seams stretch without popping?

For interlock, pique and poly-spandex shirts, we sew the main seams with 4-thread overlock and the hems and sleeve openings with 2- or 3-needle coverstitch. We use textured polyester looper thread, so the seams stretch without popping. Single-needle lockstitch is only used on plackets, collars and topstitching, and clear elastic or tape stabilises the shoulders.

How do you keep lightweight rayon or silk panels from slipping or stretching out of shape during cutting?

We take rayon and silk off the roll and let them relax for 12 to 24 hours. Then we spread them without tension over a paper underlay, in lays of no more than 30 plies, and vacuum hold-down keeps them from moving. Very fine silk is cut one ply at a time against the pattern so it keeps its shape and grain.
