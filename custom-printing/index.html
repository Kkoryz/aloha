Hawaiian Shirt Printing Options

You can choose from six printing methods for your aloha shirts: screen printing, sublimation, DTG (direct-to-garment), DTF (direct-to-film), HTV (heat transfer vinyl) and digital printing. When we consult with you, we look over your artwork and recommend the combination that fits your design.

We make the shirts ourselves in our own factory in Guangzhou, Guangdong, China.

Create Distinctive Effects with Discharge, Puff, Foil and Reverse Printing

Discharge printing

Discharge takes dye out of 100% cotton to give you a soft, vintage finish. The fabric's dye decides how much colour comes out, so we test your actual fabric lot before bulk.

Puff and foil

Puff gives you raised texture. Foil adds gold, silver or custom-colour metallic accents on cotton, polyester, rayon or blends. We use both for spot details only, because large areas stiffen the shirt and wear faster in the wash.

Reverse printing

We print on cotton so your design shows through from the back face of the fabric, which gives a muted, sun-faded look. We test the effect on your actual fabric lot before bulk.

Direct factory orders

Our factory-owned business has been running since 2011. We work directly with business buyers worldwide and have no marketplace stores, so sewing, sublimation, production and export all stay under our control.

Embroidery alongside print

Our embroidery service adds designer-prepared, wash-resistant logos, intricate designs, patches and labels. It's only available on aloha shirts we cut and sew ourselves, not on finished shirts you send us.

Which Printing Method Suits Your Logos and Artwork?

We pick a method based on how detailed your artwork is first and on order size second, because a low unit price can't make up for lost detail.

  • Screen printing

    Artwork, fabric and ordering fit
    Bold, solid-colour logos on cotton, polyester and blends, from a one-colour chest logo to a multicolour back graphic. Pantone ink matching and different press configurations support your design. Setup cost is spread across the quantity, so runs of around 500 units or more are the most economical fit.

  • Digital printing

    Artwork, fabric and ordering fit
    High-resolution, photo-realistic artwork on cotton, polyester and blends. Production is fast, which suits small and medium runs and quick-turn projects, and the prints are durable and keep shirts lightweight.

  • DTG

    Artwork, fabric and ordering fit
    Detailed, multicolour small batches on cotton and high-cotton blends, with the best results on 100% cotton. We use water-based inks and need no screen setup.

  • DTF

    Artwork, fabric and ordering fit
    Tight schedules and almost any fabric type. A single transfer step removes per-colour setup.

  • HTV

    Artwork, fabric and ordering fit
    Precision-cut names and bold one-colour logos on cotton, polyester or blends, with little setup. You can choose matte, glossy, metallic, reflective or glitter finishes.

  • Detail limits

    Artwork, fabric and ordering fit
    Screen printing holds 0.3 mm lines, 6 pt text and 45 to 55 lpi halftones. HTV needs lines of at least 1.5 mm and letters 8 mm tall, and it can't print gradients. DTG and digital hold 0.2 mm lines and DTF holds 0.5 mm lines. All three reproduce continuous-tone gradients at up to 1,200 dpi.

  • Large-run trade-off

    Artwork, fabric and ordering fit
    Choosing DTG or DTF over screen printing saves you setup and time. In exchange, you give up screen printing's lower unit cost and exact Pantone spot-colour matching.

All-Over Prints on Your Choice of Fabric

With all-over prints, your fabric decides the printing method, the colour you get and how the finished shirt drapes.

  • Sublimated polyester

    What we supply
    Sublimation bonds dye into the polyester fibres instead of leaving a layer on the surface, so it can't crack or peel. Extra colours cost nothing. We use light-coloured fabrics only.

  • Polyester blends

    What we supply
    Blends need at least 60% polyester. A 60–65% polyester blend gives about 25–30% less colour depth than 100% polyester and has a heathered, vintage finish. For the brightest result, choose 85% polyester or more.

  • Natural-fabric all-over prints

    What we supply
    We screen-print rayon, cotton, linen and silk on the roll with repeats up to 64 cm. We can also print them digitally with repeats up to the full 150 cm printable width.

  • Colour and feel

    What we supply
    Screen and digital prints on rayon and cotton keep a soft, natural hand and rich colour that comes close to sublimation brightness. Screen printing gives the deepest spot colours, and rayon keeps its fluid drape. Sublimated polyester gives up that natural softness for slightly brighter colours and a smoother, crisper, more synthetic feel.

  • Fabric-only orders

    What we supply
    Our Hawaiian Fabrics service supplies library or custom prints from 300 metres per colourway. They come in rolls of about 50 to 100 metres, each inspected for shade, defects and width and sealed on cores for export.

Plan Your Print Size, Placement and Pattern Matching

For DTG, DTF and HTV prints, we keep your artwork clear of the button placket so the shirt closes neatly.

Continuous repeat patterns

We print the fabric on the roll before cutting, so repeats match across seams, collars, plackets, pockets and hems. The trade-off is that a continuous pattern can't put a single motif in exactly the same spot on every shirt.

Fixed motif placement

We cut the panels first, print each one and then sew, so your motif sits in the same position on every shirt. Sublimation transfer on polyester is one way to do this.

DTG print areas

DTG prints up to 40 x 50 cm on your front panels, the full back, the left chest or the sleeves.

DTF transfer areas

DTF transfers go up to 50 x 60 cm and can also go on the back yoke, sleeves, pocket or near the hem.

HTV placement options

HTV covers up to 30 x 40 cm for chest, back or sleeve designs, including names and numbers.

Prepare Your Artwork, Colours and Branding for Print

  • Artwork files

    Send your artwork at 300 DPI as PNG, JPG, JPEG, PSD, PDF, AI, EPS or TIFF, or send us a reference sample. We prefer vector AI or EPS files with the text outlined and the strokes expanded.

  • File and layout review

    We check every file for clarity, durability and compliance, then show you the design layout to confirm before production starts.

  • Colour references

    Before you approve the mockup, we match your artwork, Pantone reference, hex code or approved colour sample as closely as the fabric allows. Once you approve a sample, it becomes the standard for bulk.

  • Approved care labels

    Our Custom Labels & Hang Tags service prepares content, care and origin artwork that meets the rules of your destination market, based on your actual materials and wash tests. We add your RN or CA number where it's needed, get your approval and check every private-label order.

  • Actual-fabric strike-offs

    Rayon, cotton, polyester and stretch fabrics each reproduce the same colour differently. That's why we adjust repeat, scale and placement, calibrate colours on stretch fabrics and ask you to approve a physical strike-off.

  • Branding methods

    You can choose screen printing in up to 12 colours, soft-hand DTF in unlimited colours, embroidery, heat transfer, silicone printing or sewn-on labels.

  • Garment add-ons

    Our customisation service offers placement printing, extra pockets and hi-vis tape. Placement printing and pockets are also available as add-ons on standard shirts.

Bring Your Shirt Ideas to Life with Our Design Team

  • Whole-shirt layout

    What happens for your order
    We plan the collar, short sleeves, front and back together instead of treating your design as one flat tile. That way no motifs get cut off at the seams and the finished shirt looks balanced. Your design doesn't have to be floral.

  • Starting your style

    What happens for your order
    Our product development team takes on OEM and ODM projects starting from your design, a picture or just an idea. Pattern design, grading and fit development all happen in-house, which means less back-and-forth.

  • Protecting your artwork

    What happens for your order
    Our confidentiality terms include an NDA. Patterns we develop from your artwork stay your intellectual property, and we can't sell your exclusive print to another client.

  • Coordinated production

    What happens for your order
    Under our process, we agree on price before sampling. After that, fabric, sewing, decoration and finishing all run on one timeline with one contact, and production management and quality control are coordinated across all of them. You can also talk directly with the production side.

  • Staying in contact

    What happens for your order
    Our English-speaking team is available by phone or video for your whole project and replies to enquiries within one business day.

Order Minimums and Your Production Timeline

  • Order minimum

    Orders start at 50 pieces per style in each colour, which works for small and growing labels.

  • First trial run

    We agree on your first trial quantity case by case. How flexible we can be depends on what production allows.

  • Library print trials

    Prints from our existing library have no extra minimum, so you can test a style without committing to a custom print. The catch is that the artwork isn't exclusive to your brand.

  • Custom sublimation minimum

    Custom sublimation starts at 50 pieces per print. That's the print-specific commitment for your own design.

  • Bulk production timing

    Bulk production takes 35 to 40 days after sample approval and deposit. Your order size and the season decide where it falls in that range.

  • Approved-style reorders

    Our bulk-production reorders skip sampling, keep the same minimum and take 35 to 40 days after deposit. Reprints from archived files match your original approved sample within ΔE ≤ 2.0.

Approve Your Sample and Check the Print Before Production

We make your pre-production sample in the fabric and printing method you've chosen, so you can review and approve it.

For screen-printed orders, you sign off a physical print sample before bulk printing starts. During bulk printing, we compare every roll against that approved sample. We check for white crease streaks, bleeding between neighbouring colours, misregistration and shade changes along the roll.

Long runs show defects that a single sample doesn't, so we check on the line to keep your colour consistent.

Quality Checks from Printed Fabric to Finished Shirts

We inspect your printed fabric as flat panels before cutting and sewing. We reprint anything below standard and move your order to the front of production.

  • Earlier fault correction

    Reprinting flat fabric is quicker than remaking sewn garments, so corrections don't hold up the rest of your order.

  • Inspection coverage

    Our quality control covers incoming fabric, cutting, sewing and random checks. A 100% final inspection follows and takes 2 to 3 days.

  • Pre-shipment evidence

    We sample against your approved sample at AQL 2.5 for major defects and AQL 4.0 for minor ones. We share measurement logs and photos with you, and SGS or BV inspectors are welcome.

  • Measurement tolerances

    Finished chest, length and waist measurements stay within ±1 cm of your chart. Collars, cuffs and other small points stay within ±0.5 cm.

  • Fabric performance

    Preshrunk fabrics keep shrinkage within 3% after three home washes. Washing colourfastness reaches Grade 4 or above, including on high-twist chiffon, and rubbing and light colourfastness reach Grade 3–4 or above.

  • Certification scope

    Our coverage includes OEKO-TEX Standard 100 tested fabrics and trims, GOTS organic cotton, BCI cotton sourcing, ISO 9001, WRAP, BSCI, OCS and GRS recycled polyester. Before you order, we confirm which of these apply to your style.

  • Certificate documentation

    We send current certificates on request, and for certified recycled prints we request each GRS Transaction Certificate from our certification body. For plain colours, we use OEKO-TEX certified dyes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are your screen and DTF inks plastisol or water-based, PVC- and phthalate-free, and do the prints meet OEKO-TEX or children's product requirements?

Our standard screen inks are water-based. For opaque prints on dark grounds, we offer PVC-free, phthalate-free plastisol. DTF prints use water-based pigment inks. If you need OEKO-TEX Standard 100 compliant formulations, we can supply them on request.

Within the same printing method, which artwork choices—such as print dimensions, separate print locations, spot colours or white underbases—change production cost?

Larger prints and extra locations cost you more, because each location needs its own setup and printing pass. With screen printing, each screen and spot colour has a one-off charge that's spread across your quantity. On dark shirts, a white underbase adds a screen or an ink layer. DTF, DTG and digital need no screens, so only size and locations affect the cost. We recommend screen printing for designs with few colours on longer runs, and DTF for designs with many colours on short runs. DTF saves you the screen costs, but it leaves a thin film on the surface instead of the softer, absorbed-ink feel you get from screen printing.

What positioning tolerance can you hold for a chest logo relative to the pocket or placket across the production run?

Throughout production, your chest logo stays within 0.5 cm of where it sits on your approved sample, measured from the pocket edge or placket.

What wash-durability and rubbing-colourfastness standards can you meet for screen, digital, DTG, DTF and HTV prints?

We test washing to ISO 105-C06 and rubbing to ISO 105-X12. Screen, DTF and HTV prints last at least 50 home washes at 40°C. DTG prints last at least 30 washes.
