Care label requirements for US apparel

By Huimin TangPublished 17 November 20268 min read

For ordinary US apparel that can be cleaned safely, care label requirements call for a supported washing or dry-cleaning method, necessary warnings, and instructions that stay attached and readable throughout the garment’s useful life. Fiber content, country of origin and business identification are separate garment-labeling obligations. FTC care labeling guide

At Tapa Works, we base care instructions on wash tests of your fabric and print, prepare destination-specific wording, and ask you to confirm the final label before production. A useful approval checks the wording, the assembled garment and the physical label together. For the label options that accompany your garment order, see our Custom Labels & Hang Tags page.

What your US garment care label needs to include

Your care label has to give a cleaning method that's backed by evidence for the whole garment, along with warnings against any foreseeable care that would damage it. It has to stay securely attached and legible, and people buying the garment need to be able to find it at the point of purchase. FTC care labeling guide

Run through this short checklist before you review the artwork:

  • Match the instructions to the fabric, print, seams, lining and trims as a whole.
  • If you're choosing home laundering, include both a washing and a drying method.
  • Add temperature limits and warnings wherever the garment needs them.
  • Check the attachment, that the label stays readable, and that every instruction can be reached.
  • If the packaging hides the label, repeat the care information on the package or on an attached hangtag.

You can give the instructions in English words without any symbols. A factory's stock care sentence isn't evidence that your garment will survive that procedure. The FTC accepts reliable experience or appropriate testing as support, and independent laboratory testing isn't required in every case. FTC care labeling guide

Review the proposed wording against the garment

For this illustrative proof review, picture an unlined printed rayon camp shirt with polyester sewing thread, resin buttons and a lightweight fusible collar interlining. Assume the complete garment handles cold gentle machine washing, line drying and cool ironing on the reverse. Hotter washing distorts the collar, tumble drying puckers the placket, chlorine bleach changes the print, and non-chlorine bleach is safe when needed. These are assumed inputs for the exercise.

Proposed proofRevised proof for the assumed garmentDecision log
Wash coldMachine wash cold, gentle cycleNames the washing method; the assumed collar behavior supports the restrictions.
Tumble dryLine dryReplaces the procedure that puckers the assumed placket.
Do not bleachOnly non-chlorine bleach, when neededRemoves a blanket restriction unsupported by the assumed bleach checks.

Dry clean onlyRemove this instructionThe assumed safe wash route contradicts a prohibition on washing; dry-cleaning compatibility has not been established.

Read the revised column as one complete routine: it tells you how to wash, bleach when needed, dry and press this particular shirt. The restrictions rest on the garment assumptions, while the requirement to give complete, substantiated instructions comes from the FTC care labeling guide.

The temperature and ironing fields are conditional. Washing needs a temperature limit when regular hot washing would harm the garment, and machine drying needs one when high heat would harm it. Ironing instructions belong wherever ironing is regularly needed or where foreseeable ironing calls for a warning. Leaving out a bleach restriction can permit all commercially available bleaches. Specifying line drying gives the drying route, so you don't automatically need a separate prohibition on tumble drying. FTC care labeling guide

Place care and identity information deliberately

For this shirt layout, put the origin disclosure at the inside center neck and the care instructions on an accessible side-seam label. In the artwork, keep the fiber and business information visibly separate from the care wording. A business name can identify the responsible business in place of an RN, so don't treat an RN as mandatory for every garment. These identity and origin disclosures fall under the wider garment-labeling rules. Apparel Entrepreneurship garment label requirements

The placement schedule below annotates the proposed layout. If the care text continues on the reverse, attach the label along its top edge so the reader can turn it over freely. Care text on the reverse has to be easily accessible. FTC care labeling guide

Follow the evidence from sample to approval

In an anonymized Tapa Works rayon shirt sample review, we looked at the printed shell, fusible collar, resin buttons and sewn seams together. The starting proof allowed warm washing and tumble drying. Warm washing rippled the collar and tumble drying puckered the placket, so we revised the procedure to cold gentle machine washing and line drying.

After 3 cold gentle washes with mild detergent and line drying, the revised sample showed 2% shrinkage in body length and 1% in chest width, no visible staining on the accompanying white cloth, and no loose buttons or lifted interlining. In a separate check, chlorine bleach changed the print shade, while non-chlorine bleach didn't visibly change it. A cool iron on the reverse removed wrinkles without marking the surface. The buyer approved the revised wording against the retained sample and its inspection record.

Our published fabric acceptance limit is residual shrinkage within 3% in length and width after three home washes. That's our production criterion, not a legal shrinkage allowance. We base our care wording on the tested fabric and print; see our Sampling page for the sample stage and see our Quality Control page for inspection support.

Notice what each observation actually establishes. The measurements address dimensional change, the white cloth checks for visible transfer, and the collar and button observations concern the assembled components. A successful wash on its own doesn't prove bleach tolerance or dry-cleaning compatibility. The evidence has to support the instructions for the whole garment, including how the components interact. FTC care labeling guide

Inspect the label after care

For our label-format comparison, we used matching printed rayon panels with two formats: screen-printed polyester satin labels sewn along the top edge, and heat-transfer care artwork applied directly to the fabric. Each format carried a 30 mm by 60 mm artwork area. We photographed the dry panels before care and again after 10 cold gentle machine washes with mild detergent and line drying, using the same lighting and viewing distance.

Label format and attachmentBefore careAfter the comparison trial
Screen-printed satin, sewn top edgeAll words readable; no visible fading; seam secureAll words readable; slight ink fading; no seam opening or detachment
Heat transfer, bonded to rayonAll words readable; edges fully bondedAll words readable; fine surface cracking; no edge lifting or detachment

These observations apply to the compared samples and conditions. A finite wash trial doesn't establish readability over the garment's entire useful life. Choosing the material isn't enough on its own either: the print, application and attachment all need inspecting. The US care rule doesn't prescribe a universal label material, font type or font height. Cotecna US care labeling guide

What are the five basic international care symbols?

The five basic care-symbol families are washing, bleaching, drying, ironing and professional cleaning, shown as a tub, a triangle, a square, an iron and a circle. For a US label, though, the family shapes are only the starting point: the instructions have to be supported and use either the permitted ASTM system or complete English wording. BIORESTORE care label guide; FTC care labeling guide

FamilyBasic shapeWhat to read
WashingTubWashing method, temperature and cycle restrictions.
BleachingTriangleWhether bleach is allowed and any restriction on type.
DryingSquareNatural drying method or tumble-drying conditions.
IroningIronWhether ironing is allowed and its heat restriction.
Professional cleaningStandalone circleProfessional cleaning route and process restrictions.

Dots modify the heat setting, bars can indicate gentler treatment, and crosses prohibit the procedure shown. Read those modifiers using the specified symbol system. A circle inside a square relates to tumble drying, while a standalone circle relates to professional cleaning. Natural drying uses other marks inside the square. BIORESTORE care label guide; FTC care labeling guide

Translate the whole proof, then find conflicts

Go back to the assumed rayon shirt. Its washing family needs cold gentle-machine care, its bleaching family needs a non-chlorine restriction, its drying family needs line drying, and its ironing family needs low heat with reverse-side wording. The exercise has no supported professional-cleaning instruction. Knowing the families doesn't mean every label needs an icon from each one: a supported wash route can be enough. FTC care labeling guide

For US artwork, specify the ASTM edition named in the FTC's care labeling guide rather than assuming an ISO/GINETEX row is interchangeable. The same guide also says a standalone professional-wetclean instruction doesn't meet its requirements. If symbol selection is still unresolved, complete English instructions give you a practical route. FTC care labeling guide

Match modifiers to component behavior

In the rayon shirt case above, we swapped the unrestricted wash tub for the cold gentle-wash variant because the hotter procedure rippled the fused collar. We replaced tumble drying with line drying because of the placket puckering, chose the non-chlorine bleach restriction based on the print checks, and kept the low-heat iron with reverse-side wording based on the pressing check. We removed an unsubstantiated dry-cleaning symbol rather than treating the rayon shell as proof that the assembled shirt could be dry cleaned.

The key question is which component controls the instruction. A shell fabric may tolerate a procedure that damages its print, interfacing or attached trim. That's why your supporting records need to address the assembled garment, even where component tests contribute evidence. FTC care labeling guide

Check the marks that change the meaning

In the same label-format trial, the original symbol outlines were 4 mm high in both the artwork and the printed samples. Before washing, the heat dots, gentleness bars and prohibition crosses were distinct on both formats. After the recorded wash trial, the satin print still had distinct modifiers, but a crack running through a heat-transfer dot made its boundary ambiguous under magnification. We enlarged the revised symbols to 5 mm and held that artwork for a fresh sample review; the enlarged version had no completed wash result.

Keep matched macro photographs alongside the general readability photographs. Judge each dot, bar and cross on its own: a readable sentence won't rescue a contradictory or broken symbol. Enlarging the artwork is a revision you still need to verify, not proof that the production problem has gone away.

Use the inspection sheet above to record the revised file and its approval status. Check the printed sample at actual size as well as under magnification. Confirm the complete routine against the garment evidence, then make sure no modifier has disappeared into the weave, a print gap or the seam.

Before you approve your factory's care label

Approve the care label as a package: the final proof, the garment-specific supporting evidence, the placement record and the inspected production sample. Sort out any unsupported restrictions, conflicting symbols and unreadable instructions before you release the artwork for production.

We draft labels from the fabric and trims used, send you the wording to confirm, and check the labels on private-label orders. Send your garment specification and proposed care artwork along with your brief; see our Private Label page for how labels fit into your collection. Keep the approved label and the garment sample together so the production team can check both content and construction against the same decision.
