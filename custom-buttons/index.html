Shirt Buttons for Your Collection

Resin buttons make it easier to keep size, colour and supply consistent from your first bulk order through every reorder.

We supply aloha shirt buttons in resin, urea, plastic, shell, coconut, wood, metal and fabric-covered types to suit your design, fabric and order. In resin, you can choose solid colours, horn-like or pearl-like finishes, or a custom colour you specify.

Our button makers produce the custom buttons, and we sew them onto shirts made in our own single factory in Guangzhou, Guangdong, China.

Button Materials, Finishes and Hidden Fastenings

  • Resin and plastic

    What we supply and what your choice means
    Resin buttons are cast from polyester resin. Plastic buttons are injection-moulded in ABS, nylon or acrylic and come in white, black, navy and clear.

  • Imitation shell

    What we supply and what your choice means
    If your volume range needs a uniform pearly look, we offer polyester resin layered with pearlescent pigment. Its lustre and colour stay the same from batch to batch. The trade-off is that you lose the natural iridescence and premium feel of genuine shell.

  • Genuine shell

    What we supply and what your choice means
    For private label and higher-end casual collections, genuine shell gives you a depth and play of light that resin can't fully copy. The trade-off is that colour won't be perfectly uniform across large runs.

  • Shell varieties

    What we supply and what your choice means
    Trochus is dense and white, with a soft pink-veined lustre. Freshwater mother-of-pearl has a milky white sheen. Black-lip shows grey to dark iridescence, and abalone gives a vivid green-blue play of colour.

  • Urea

    What we supply and what your choice means
    You can choose polished, smooth or horn-like surfaces.

  • Coconut

    What we supply and what your choice means
    Coconut buttons are available for resort, tropical, linen and casual summer shirts.

  • Wood

    What we supply and what your choice means
    Before bulk production starts, we check the washing method, coating and durability of the wooden buttons.

  • Metal

    What we supply and what your choice means
    Finishes include shiny, matte, brushed, antique and black. Lightweight fabric can't hold metal buttons or snaps on its own, so those need backing support.

  • Fabric-covered

    What we supply and what your choice means
    We make covered buttons from your matching, printed or contrast fabric.

  • Hidden snaps

    What we supply and what your choice means
    Snaps fitted beneath a covered placket keep the fastenings out of sight and hold the front flat and closed so it doesn't gape. This works especially well on women's styles, fitted cuts and uniform aloha shirts. We test snap positions on your samples and use the placement you confirm in bulk production.

Factory Ordering and Sourcing Options

Shirt order minimum

Orders start at 50 pieces per style in each colour, so small and growing labels have a set quantity to start from.

First trial run

We agree each first trial run case by case, and production capacity sets its quantity.

Direct factory ordering

As a factory-owned B2B business, we handle both production and export, which gives you direct control over quality, cost and delivery. Orders are agreed through our website and team, not through an online shop or third-party marketplace.

Your development starting point

OEM and ODM development can start from your own design, a picture or an idea for a new shirt style.

Sourcing without garment production

Standalone Fabric Sourcing starts at 300 metres per colour for stock or custom-dyed fabrics, and the trim minimums for garment orders still apply.

Materials are inspected at our factory and shipped with reports and photos to your factory anywhere in the world, by express, air or sea, on FOB, CIF or DDP terms.

Button Colour Matching and Logo Details

  • Matched or contrasting colours

    Our button maker dyes resin or plastic to match your fabric, or sets a contrast in a dark, light, horn-like or brand colour. The shade is confirmed before production, so you have an agreed reference for reorders.

  • Natural material trade-off

    Genuine shell, coconut and wood keep their natural tones and aren't custom dye-matched. Shell comes in white, smoked or dark tones, and wood in a light, medium or dark stain. An exact-match resin or plastic button loses their organic texture and grain.

  • Surface finish choices

    Depending on the material you pick, you can choose glossy, matte, smooth, horn-like, pearl-like, wood grain or engraved finishes.

  • Sample logo checks

    Before bulk production, we check the engraving depth, the button colour and how clearly your mark reads on the sample.

  • Laser engraving options

    Your logo can be laser-engraved on resin, urea, wood, coconut and shell buttons, using a method that suits the material.

  • Moulded and metal marks

    For branded detail, logos can be moulded into resin and plastic buttons, or die-stamped or engraved on metal buttons.

  • Coloured logo printing

    If your design calls for a coloured mark, we pad-print logos in one or two colours on resin, plastic and urea buttons.

Button Sizes, Hole Patterns and Stitching

  • Front placket

    Sizes, construction and checks
    For the front placket, you can choose 18L (approx. 11.5 mm) or 20L (approx. 12.5 mm).

  • Chest pockets

    Sizes, construction and checks
    Pockets take 16L (approx. 10 mm) or 18L buttons, so they stay in proportion with the front.

  • Collars and cuffs

    Sizes, construction and checks
    We scale collar and cuff buttons down from the front-placket size: 14L to 16L (9 to 10 mm) on the collar and 16L to 18L (10 to 11.5 mm) on the cuffs.

  • Alternative sizes

    Sizes, construction and checks
    If you want a different proportion, you can pick any size from 14L to 24L. The front, collar and cuffs don't have to share the same size.

  • Button thickness

    Sizes, construction and checks
    We choose the thickness to suit your fabric weight, your shirt style and how the shirt will be worn.

  • Buttonhole fit

    Sizes, construction and checks
    We develop button size and buttonhole length together, using the actual button chosen for each position, and we confirm the fit on your sample. If a hole is cut for a different diameter or thickness, the button slips out or is hard to push through, and the hole wears faster.

  • Holes and stitching

    Sizes, construction and checks
    You can have two-hole buttons, or four-hole buttons sewn with a cross stitch, a parallel stitch or another pattern you approve. A cross stitch gives a neat finish and helps the buttons stay on through regular wear and washing. On heavy-use shirts, we confirm the thread quality and sewing strength.

  • Garment assembly

    Sizes, construction and checks
    With our Cut & Sew service, your size-specific shirt panels go through assembly, buttonholes, button attachment, labelling, pressing and individual polybag packing, with inline checks along the way and a final inspection at the end.

Spare Buttons for Customer Care

We can include one or more spare buttons with every shirt to support your after-sales service.

If the spare is sewn inside the garment, or attached to or beside the care label, it stays with the shirt for its whole life, but the inside of the shirt is no longer as neat and unmarked.

If it's packed separately in a small bag or polybag, or attached with the hang tag, the inside stays neat and unmarked, but when that packaging is thrown away, the spare goes with it.

For retail shirts, sewing the spare into the garment keeps a replacement within your customer's reach.

Button Costs, Tooling and Delivery Terms

  • Stored moulds for reorders

    We keep moulds for 3 years. If a design is unchanged, we reuse its mould across seasons, so it goes straight into button production with no new tooling cost or development time.

  • Deposit and balance

    You pay a 50% deposit when you confirm the order and the remaining 50% before shipment.

  • Freight and delivery terms

    We ship on FOB, CIF or DDP terms. DDP delivery is available worldwide, with duties, customs and freight in one price.

  • Trim cost differences

    Our Trims & Accessories pricing lists each upgrade above stock resin/coconut, along with added trims and reinforcements, and one-time tooling is paid together with the deposit.

  • Buyer-supplied material allowances

    Your supplied trims and materials arrive before cutting, with 3% spare trims/labels and 5–8% extra fabric on top of our calculated consumption.

  • Supplied button specifications

    Buttons you supply need two or four holes at least 1.5 mm wide. We sew them onto your sample to confirm the buttonhole fit and check that they're attached securely.

  • Incoming supplied-material checks

    We check each delivery against your trim sheet and report any shortages or defects within 2 business days, before the materials go into production.

Button Selection, Minimums and Quality Checks

  1. Set your requirements

    Before sampling or bulk production, we pick button options based on your fabric, shirt style, quantity, preferred buttons, logo details and packing requirements.

  2. Start with stock

    On a small or first run, a colour-matched stock button works at the garment minimum. It has no separate button minimum and no extra cost. The trade-off is that you don't get a branded logo or an exact custom shade.

  3. Commit to custom lots

    Custom-dyed or logo-engraved buttons need 1,000 pieces per design in each colour, which is a minimum the button maker sets. Ordering that separate lot means you lose the low commitment of a small test order.

  4. Keep surplus for reorders

    We store the spare buttons from your custom lot for your reorders, so you can keep using them once your style's volumes justify the separate button order.

  5. Approve production details

    At sampling, we review the material, colour, size, thickness and sewing method with you. Only the button details you approve go into bulk production.

  6. Check quality and compliance

    Our Quality Control covers inspections, function testing and access to SGS/BV. Our credentials cover tested fabrics and trims (OEKO-TEX Standard 100), organic cotton (GOTS), cotton sourcing (BCI), quality management (ISO 9001) and social compliance (WRAP).

Frequently Asked Questions

Will natural shell, coconut or wood buttons vary from piece to piece and between reorders, and how do you keep them consistent?

Yes. Natural shell, coconut and wood vary slightly in colour, grain and pattern between pieces and between batches. For reorders, consistency means staying within your approved sample range. It doesn't mean every button looks identical. We sort shades against your sample so each batch stays within that range.

What usable engraving area and minimum text or line sizes can you achieve on each button size?

On an 18L (11.5 mm) four-hole shirt button, you can engrave a ring about 2 mm wide around the holes. Shank and snap buttons give you a centre area of about 6 mm, and that grows to about 10 mm on 24L (15 mm) buttons and larger. For laser engraving, text and lines need to be at least 0.8 mm high and 0.2 mm wide. Moulded marks need to be at least 1.2 mm high. A simple mark, monogram or short wordmark works better on shirt-size buttons than full brand artwork. Fine lines and small text fill in or blur around the holes, and that makes the mark hard to read.

Can I order shank buttons or custom-shaped buttons instead of standard round two-hole or four-hole buttons?

Yes. Besides round two-hole and four-hole buttons, we supply shank buttons, snaps and specialty styles, cut or moulded in square, oval, flower, leaf and logo-outline shapes from your drawing. Then we confirm the shape on your sample before bulk production.

Keeping shell, coconut and wood buttons close to the approved sample.

  1. Confirm sample variation

    Slight variation in colour, grain and pattern is normal and confirmed on your sample.

  2. Keep approved references

    Buttons from your approved sample are kept as the reference.

  3. Hand-sort each batch

    Each batch is hand-sorted by shade against the approved reference.

  4. Set aside outliers

    Pieces outside the approved range are set aside.
