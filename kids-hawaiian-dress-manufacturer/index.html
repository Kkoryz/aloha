Kids' Hawaiian Dress Manufacturer in China

For toddlers up to age 4, cotton poplin and cotton-spandex jersey give your kids’ Hawaiian dresses fabrics that are durable and easy to wear, while for girls aged 5 to 14, lawn, voile and rayon challis give the skirt a flowing shape.

For kids' dresses, we work with cotton poplin (110–130 gsm), lighter cotton lawn and voile (70–90 gsm), rayon challis (100–130 gsm) and soft 95% cotton 5% spandex jersey (160–180 gsm). For performance, you can choose quick-dry polyester or 65% polyester 35% cotton (120–140 gsm).

We make the dresses ourselves, in our single, company-owned factory in Guangzhou, Guangdong, China.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which lightweight or pale-coloured fabrics provide adequate coverage without lining, and what lining options do you offer for sheer choices?

For most prints, cotton poplin from 110 gsm and rayon challis from 120 gsm give enough coverage without a lining. White and pastel grounds, and lawn or voile under 90 gsm, need lining. For those sheer choices, we line the bodice or the whole dress with 100% cotton batiste at 60–70 gsm or a soft polyester lining at 70–80 gsm, and you pick a matching or white shade at sampling. Before we confirm the lining, we check a sewn sample in your actual print and ground colour against daylight and again after washing. Show-through depends on the specific print, colour and seams, so a flat swatch on its own doesn't tell you how well the finished dress covers.

How do your cotton and performance fabrics differ in skirt drape and bulk at gathered waist seams?

Rayon challis and cotton lawn drape softly and gather into a flat, low-bulk waist seam, which suits full and tiered skirts. Cotton poplin holds a crisper A-line shape and adds bulk at the gathers. Polyester and cotton-spandex knits hang smoothly with less volume. With fabrics heavier than 150 gsm, we bring the gather ratio down to about 1.5:1 and overlock the waist seam so it lies flat against the body. Before we confirm gather fullness, we look at the sewn sample in your actual print and ground colour against daylight and after a wash, because the waist seam’s bulk shows in the made-up dress, not just in a flat swatch.

How do fabric width and one-way tropical print layouts affect material consumption and cost for full-skirted children's dresses?

Our 145–150 cm fabric width fits gathered or circle skirt panels for sizes up to 8 across the width. A one-way tropical print, though, takes 5–10% more fabric than a two-way layout, because we can't turn the pieces head-to-tail. Large repeats or pattern matching use 10–15% more fabric, and we build that extra consumption into your unit price.

Do any performance fabrics offered for these dresses have a verified UPF rating, and which fabric and colour combinations does that rating cover?

We offer a UPF rating such as UPF 50+ on quick-dry polyester-spandex woven and stretch poplin only when a laboratory report to AS/NZS 4399 or EN 13758-1 backs it up, and we send that report with your order. The rating covers only the fabric quality and colour named in that report, not every fabric or shade in the range. Each colourway you want to label with UPF is tested separately before production.

Can you combine different fabrics for the bodice and skirt, and which combinations are unsuitable because of stretch, weight or wash behaviour?

Yes, we combine fabrics in one dress, for example a woven cotton poplin bodice with a knit or rayon challis skirt, or a 95% cotton 5% spandex stretch bodice with a cotton gauze skirt. A heavy knit skirt on a lightweight voile or gauze bodice drags the waist seam, so that pairing is unsuitable, and joining a high-stretch knit to rigid poplin needs a stabilised seam. At sampling, we wash-test every combination to check that the bodice and skirt shrink and recover evenly. Before we confirm the pairing, we look at the sewn dress in your actual print and ground colour against daylight and after washing, because uneven shrinkage between the two parts only shows once the garment is made up and washed.
