Garment Dyed Shirts From Our Own Factory

Garment dyeing starts with shirts sewn from PFD (prepared for dye) fabric, the standard base for this process.

We make your aloha shirts in our own factory in Guangzhou, Guangdong, China. The certified dyestuffs and silicone softeners we use are free from heavy metals, formaldehyde and banned azo compounds, and they meet OEKO-TEX Standard 100 Class I requirements.

Before you order, we confirm which certificate covers each dye, softener and fabric. On request, we send you current copies so you can check them against your market's requirements.

The right dye for each fabric

Before we choose a dye route, we confirm your fabric's exact fibre content from the mill specification.

  • Dye classes

    What it means for your shirts
    Cotton, rayon, linen and bamboo take reactive dyes. Silk, nylon and wool take acid dyes. Polyester and polyester blends take disperse dyes.

  • Stretch blends

    What it means for your shirts
    We garment dye cotton or rayon with up to 5% spandex, and nylon with up to 20% spandex. We control bath temperatures to protect the stretch, and nylon-spandex takes acid dyes.

  • Mixed fibres

    What it means for your shirts
    Any mix of cotton, rayon and linen dyes evenly. A 65% polyester 35% cotton blend turns two-tone in a single bath, so it needs two-step disperse and reactive dyeing for uniform colour, and we agree this with you before sampling.

  • Threads and trims

    What it means for your shirts
    Your tech pack lists the finished shade of each thread and trim, and we test thread against fabric at sampling. Cotton thread dyes tone-on-tone with cotton, rayon or linen. Polyester thread, coconut or shell buttons and polyester woven labels keep their original colour.

  • Rayon challis

    What it means for your shirts
    This 110–130 gsm PFD base gives classic aloha styles a fluid, loose resort drape.

  • Cotton poplin

    What it means for your shirts
    This 115–135 gsm PFD base gives tailored, uniform and camp-collar shirts a crisper structure.

  • Linen or linen-cotton

    What it means for your shirts
    These 150–180 gsm PFD bases give washed summer collections a textured, relaxed vintage feel.

  • Factory ownership

    What it means for your shirts
    As a factory-owning company, we handle production and export ourselves, so quality, cost and delivery stay under our direct control.

  • Style development

    What it means for your shirts
    Our OEM and ODM product development works from your design, picture or idea. Pattern design, grading and fit development all happen in-house, which cuts down the back-and-forth.

  • Fabric range

    What it means for your shirts
    Our shirt fabrics cover cotton, polyester, spandex, nylon, rayon, viscose, modal, Tencel, linen, silk and wool, plus 95% cotton 5% spandex, 80% nylon 20% spandex and 65% polyester 35% cotton blends.

Choose Your Shade and Set the Colour Standard

  • Base and colour together

    You pick a standard colour, a physical swatch or a Pantone reference. We then check that your chosen fibre reaches that shade before you commit to the colour standard.

  • Natural-fibre colour depth

    Cotton, rayon and linen get their deepest tropical colours from reactive dyes, and direct dyes give them pale and pastel options. On silk and nylon, acid dyes give both brights and pastels.

  • Softer colour limits

    In garment-dye baths, polyester and polyester-rich blends reach light to mid shades, not the deepest brights. Pigment dyeing gives softer, washed-down tones.

  • Repeat-order variation

    Your reorder comes out very close to the earlier run but not identical to it, because batch-to-batch variation is part of garment dyeing.

  • Lot acceptance standard

    Shirts in the same bath match to Grade 4–5 on the grey scale. To pass, a lot must be within ΔE ≤ 2.0 of your approved sample under D65 light. We re-dye or set aside any outliers and send you comparison photos before shipment.

  • Colourfastness minimums

    Custom-dyed cotton and rayon reach Grade 4 or above for washing and Grade 3–4 or above for rubbing and light. Pigment-dyed shirts meet the same minimums.

  • Dark-colour lab checks

    For navy, black and deep red, we use reactive dyes. Before you approve bulk, we check washing fastness and wet and dry rubbing fastness on the lab dip.

  • Fixing deep shades

    Deep, saturated shades are the hardest to hold against rubbing, so dark colours get a colour-fixing after-treatment.

Finishes that shape the feel

Three finish choices

Choose solid garment dye if you want colour that stays rich wash after wash, washed if you want a washed finish, or pigment-dyed if you want colour that fades with wear.

Softer shirt hand

Every garment-dyed shirt goes through a softening wash. We use light or heavy concentrations of silicone softener to give your shirts a super-soft, silky feel for many washes.

Wash and vintage effects

On top of softening, we offer enzyme, stone, acid and mineral washes, sun-faded and vintage effects, and light distressing. You approve each effect on a sample first.

Individual dye patterns

We also do dip-dye and tie-dye patterns, which you approve on a sample first. These finishes make each shirt slightly different from the next.

Decoration timing matters

Embroidery and patches go on before dyeing to give a tone-on-tone vintage look. Placement screen prints and puff prints go on after dyeing so the graphics stay sharp.

Plan for Shrinkage and Keep the Fit

  • Finished measurements

    What we do
    Your spec sheet gives the dimensions after dyeing. We cut the patterns oversized so the shirts shrink to the measurements you intended.

  • Pattern allowances

    What we do
    We allow 3–5% for cotton poplin, 5–8% for rayon and 4–6% for linen. A wash test on your sample fabric sets the exact figure.

  • Fabric changes

    What we do
    Whenever you change the fabric base or supplier, we run a new wash test and adjust the pattern, even if the fibre stays the same.

  • Residual shrinkage

    What we do
    Your shirts leave us dyed, washed and pre-shrunk. After three home washes, residual shrinkage stays within 3% in length and width.

  • Construction

    What we do
    During Cut & Sew sampling, we decide whether your collar and placket get lightweight fusible interlining, soft non-fused interlining or none. For lightweight rayon, we use 1 cm overlocked or French seams, tested to ISO 13936-2 to open no more than 6 mm.

Colour approvals and style details

  • Your actual fabric

    We make lab dips on your actual shirt fabric, not on generic test cloth. The colour you approve becomes the bulk colour standard on the base you chose.

  • Included colour approval

    Pantone matching costs nothing extra, and you get one round of lab dips to approve before bulk production starts.

  • Coordinated style colours

    You pick from Pantone Coated cards or our in-stock colour cards, and we coordinate the colour across your fabric, lining and trims.

  • Labels for your market

    With Custom Labels & Hang Tags, we write the fibre content, origin and wash-tested care instructions to your destination's rules and add RN or CA numbers where they're required. You approve the artwork, and we check every private-label order.

  • Printed collection options

    Custom Printing covers roll printing on natural fabrics and sublimation on light polyester or on blends with at least 60% polyester. Screen repeats go up to 64 cm, and digital and sublimation repeats run the full 150 cm. Large repeats or pattern matching take 10–15% more fabric, and that cost is already in the price.

  • Matching across fabric bases

    For Matching Family & Group Sets, the same Pantone looks different on polyester, rayon and cotton, so you approve each base side by side. We scale the artwork from toddler sizes up to 5XL and sample the whole set together.

  • Button order quantities

    Custom Buttons need 1,000 pieces per design in each colour, whether custom-dyed or logo-engraved. We order them separately and store the spares for your reorders. Stock colour matches have no separate minimum, so they work with the 50-piece shirt minimum.

From first sample to production

All garment production happens in our own factory, garment dyeing included, while the PFD base fabric comes from our partner mills.

  1. Review your brief

    Before we quote, we confirm whether you want garment dyeing or pigment dyeing and review the substrate in your brief or tech pack.

  2. Settle colour decisions

    We agree the colour references and get your approval on the swatch or lab dip before sampling. Lab dips add about 5 to 7 days to sampling.

  3. Make your sample

    A standard sample takes 10 to 15 days, and you get the shirt to review before you commit to bulk production.

  4. Release bulk production

    Bulk production starts once you approve the sample and pay the deposit. The base schedule is 35 to 40 days, depending on order size and season.

  5. Dye and finish

    Garment dyeing adds 7 to 10 days to that bulk schedule for the extra dyeing and finishing work on your shirts.

  6. Inspect and dispatch

    When production is finished, your order goes through final inspection before we release the shirts for shipment.

Quotes and quantities for your order

  • Minimum and bath size

    Our minimum is 50 pieces per style in each colour. That fills one bath of about 10 kg, or roughly 50 short-sleeve shirts. There's no separate dye-bath minimum.

  • Shared colours together

    Order styles that share a colour at the same time and they go into the same bath. Larger quantities run across several baths of up to 100 kg each.

  • First trial quantities

    We agree first-trial quantities case by case, with as much flexibility as production allows for your order.

  • Price before sampling

    In our order process, we quote first. Once you agree the price, we start making your sample.

  • Direct factory contact

    Our English-speaking contact team replies within one business day, and you can reach them by phone or video call throughout your order.

Costs, payments and checks before dispatch

  • Sample and design fees

    You pay USD 50 for each sample and USD 50 for each design, which covers its pattern or print in all colourways. Neither fee is refunded or credited against your bulk order.

  • Payment split

    You pay a 50% deposit when you confirm the order and the remaining 50% before shipment.

  • Shipping terms

    We ship on FOB, CIF or DDP terms. DDP delivery is available worldwide, with duties, customs and freight in one price.

  • Sample courier cost

    Sample delivery by courier costs USD 50, and you pay this on top of the sample fee.

  • Pre-dispatch quality checks

    Our Quality Control covers a 100% final inspection over 2 to 3 days and pre-shipment sampling at AQL 2.5 for major defects and 4.0 for minor ones.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you overdye an already printed aloha shirt, or garment dye blank shirts that we send you?

We overdye aloha shirts that we've printed in our own factory. The print softens into the new ground colour and gives a sun-faded, worn-in look. We only garment dye shirts we cut and sew ourselves, so we don't take in your blank or finished shirts for dyeing.

How many standard garment dye colours do you have, and can I get a physical colour card before I choose?

Our standard range has 30 shades developed on our PFD shirt fabrics, from washed neutrals and earth tones to ocean blues, tropical brights and deep darks. If you ask, we post you a physical colour card with dyed swatches within 3 business days.

If my order needs more than one dye lot, how do you stop shirts of slightly different shades ending up mixed together on the shelf?

We pack each dye lot separately by shade batch and mark every polybag and carton with its lot number, so different shade batches never share a carton. Every lot also gets its own entry on the packing list, which lets you store, shelve and ship lot by lot.

Which of my garment dyeing choices make the shirt cost more or less?

Your quote itemises each choice: the dye route (reactive, acid, disperse or pigment), shade depth, two-step dyeing for mixed fibres, extra washes such as enzyme, stone or tie-dye, and heavy or light silicone softener. Runs close to the minimum of 50 pieces per colour per dye lot cost more per shirt than full production runs.

What kinds of aloha shirt collections is garment dyeing best for, and can I hold undyed shirts and dye colours as sales come in?

We sew the shirts before we colour them, so garment dyeing suits vintage and lived-in resort lines, tonal solids and core styles you offer in many colours. We hold your sewn, undyed PFD shirts for up to 6 months, and you call off colours as sales develop, starting at 50 pieces per colour per dye lot. Each lot takes 7 to 10 days.

What quality problems typically come up with garment-dyed shirts, and what do you do to prevent them?

The main risks are shrinkage and shade variation, so we compensate the patterns for shrinkage and pack shirts by shade batch. To prevent uneven dyeing and streaks, we scour every shirt before dyeing and load the machine at a fixed fabric-to-water ratio.
